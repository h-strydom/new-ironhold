Gold means very little on this continent. There is nobody out here to spend it on, and nothing it buys that matters more than staying alive.

What [[New Ironhold]] pays in is **Gilda**: cotton notes, not coin, issued by the expedition and its companies against completed work. The crossing is rough on cargo and coin is heavy, so paper was the government's answer to a hold that could not afford the weight.

A note is worth exactly its face in the old coin: 1₲ is a copper piece, 10₲ a silver, 100₲ a gold and 1,000₲ a platinum. Notes are printed in each of those sizes and one more, the 10,000₲ note, worth ten platinum, so that a big payout does not arrive as a brick of paper.

## What it is for

Gear, information and favours: whatever [[New Ironhold]] can provide, or whatever the mainland can once a ship arrives. Take a job from [Quests](/a/cmt21jyb000033zod4t3x92rk/nj5mzdvg), finish it, get paid by whoever posted it.

## What it is not for

Gilda does not replace what you find in the wild. A blade recovered from a ruin is still a blade, and a rare reagent is worth trading whether or not it is ever sold for notes.

It does not buy levels either. Advancement comes from the work you finish and what you bring back.

## What a job pays

Most posted work pays 10,000₲ to 20,000₲ per party, split however you like, scaling with how dangerous the job is.

## Prices are not what you would expect

Nothing here is priced the way it would be at home. It is priced by what is scarce.

Food costs more than you think, because there is no farmland yet worth the name. Arrows are cheap, because the wilderness supplies the wood. A smith will mend a cracked breastplate for almost nothing, but a new one costs dearly: the skill to repair is local, and the metal to forge from scratch is not.

Haggle. Take hand-me-downs. Trade what you found for what you need. That is how it works out here.
