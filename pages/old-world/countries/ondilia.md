A theocracy: the seat is an office of the faith, not anyone's right, and the temples fill it from within. Each temple names one of its priests to the rank above it, rank names rank, and whoever stands at the top of that ladder is the delegate for as long as the office is theirs. Nobody outside the priesthood has a hand in it. The ladder is hierarchical and it is corrupt: a priest rises because the priest above him chose him, and a priest above him is owed for it. Nobody in Ondilia goes hungry, and only the one at the top lives well.

## Seat

The delegate answers to the temples and to nobody else. Because rank is given from above, the delegate is the product of every favour owed on the way up, and the priesthood below owes its own place to the same chain. Nothing is voted on inside the faith and nothing is appealed outside it. The seat does not pass at a death or a fall so much as at the next name spoken from above, and the temples do not discuss in public how that name is reached.

## Land and trade

Rich temperate farmland, the mainland's granary, and large. Ondilia grew by absorbing its neighbours, and under the assembly's rule that costs a seat for every country absorbed. How it did so is not decided. It has stopped on purpose, and does not take more. The granary is why nobody starves. Hunger is not how the priesthood keeps its hold, and the fed are not the comfortable: below the top, a priest eats, sleeps under a roof and keeps what the temple allows him, and the surplus runs upward.

Ondilia is where the mainland's science is done. The temples are its laboratories and its libraries, and a great deal of what the continent knows about nature was worked out by priests with a vow, a bench and a cellar of books. The science is natural philosophy, medicine and alchemy, not machines. Paper, ink and copied books are what that work produces, and they are what Ondilia sells. It buys in paper stock and pigment to make them.

The same priesthood sets the sailing calendar. A priest rules each year which days a ship may not leave harbour, and no master sails against it. The calendar is worked out, not guessed, which is why the masters keep it.

## Ashvale

Ashvale lies against its border. Ashvale's poor cross over for work in Ondilia's fields and temple workshops, and are taken on cheaply, because they have nowhere better to go. The border is not closed to them, and Ondilia's own granary stays full on their labour.

## What the DM can use

- Ondilia sent nobody to New Ironhold and expects nothing from it. It has no stake in the expedition and no standing claim on anything found there.
- The seat is the one on the assembly with an obvious price in favours. A delegate who needs something from a priest below him cannot easily ask for it.
- Ondilia's temples are the likeliest place on the mainland for a copy of any text, a pigment recipe or a record of Ashvale reagents, which gives a reason for a book, a map or a letter from there to turn up in play.
- A ship that is late, held back or sailed on a forbidden day is a fact about Ondilia's priesthood, not about the weather.
